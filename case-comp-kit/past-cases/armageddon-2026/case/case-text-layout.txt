Case Problem

Ironbridge Industrial Group
Established in 1978 and headquartered in Pune, Ironbridge Industrial Group manufactures precision auto
components, industrial equipment and machinery, and specialty materials and coatings. Its customers include
vehicle OEMs, capital-goods manufacturers, renewable-energy equipment makers and general engineering
firms.
The three businesses serve different customer needs. Auto Components, the largest revenue contributor,
supplies precision shafts, housings and brackets. Customers require dimensional accuracy, component
traceability, reliable delivery and competitive pricing. Production combines forging, machining and assembly.
Industrial Equipment & Machinery supplies industrial pumps, drive modules and custom assemblies. Its
customers value equipment reliability, configuration flexibility, lead times and service support. Specialty Materials
& Coatings produces protective coatings and specialty compounds through mixing, batch processing and coating
operations, with customers emphasising batch consistency, application performance and technical support.
An international production network
Ironbridge operates 11 plants: five in Auto Components, three in Industrial Equipment & Machinery and three in
Specialty Materials & Coatings. Six plants are in India, two in Vietnam and three in Central Europe. Europe is its
largest export market, followed by North America and Southeast Asia.
This footprint places different production environments within the same Group. Component lines, equipment
assembly and batch processing must each meet their customers’ requirements while coordinating production,
materials and delivery. The new CEO must consider where a Group-wide approach is appropriate and where
individual businesses require a different approach.
The digital starting point
Digital infrastructure is uneven across the network. Eight plants use a shared ERP, while three rely on local
systems and monthly manual consolidation. Centrally accessible machine-level data are available for 40% of
production equipment; other equipment relies on local logs or manual records. Data definitions also vary across
plants.
A central team of 24 employees covers data engineering, analytics, architecture and programme delivery. Plant
teams remain involved in day-to-day use of the systems: maintenance staff respond to alerts, quality teams
review flagged defects and planners approve scheduling recommendations. The existing programme therefore
sits across central technology capabilities and local operating practices.
Ironbridge and all company data are fictional. Financial amounts are in USD million unless stated otherwise;
financial years end on 31 December.

Digital transformation and board mandate

Factory of the Future
In 2023, Ironbridge launched its debt-funded Factory of the Future programme. Predictive maintenance was
intended to reduce unplanned downtime, computer-vision inspection to improve quality, and an agentic supply-
chain platform to improve planning and inventory management. The initiatives were deployed across selected
machines, lines and plants rather than the full production network.
The projects are at different stages of deployment. Maintenance alerts are available in production, but work-order
integration remains incomplete. Vision inspection covers selected product families and still requires manual
review of some flagged rejects. The planning platform recommends schedules and replenishment actions, with
planners retaining approval authority.
The board now wants to understand how the programme fits the needs of the wider business. Its review must
consider the existing investments alongside opportunities beyond those deployments, including how digital
capabilities could support Ironbridge’s customer proposition and future growth. The exhibits provide the financial,
operating and project data for this assessment.

Management perspectives
Management has different views on the challenges facing Ironbridge. The COO points to input and energy costs,
while the Auto Components sales head highlights OEM pricing and changing product demand. Plant managers
raise uneven adoption of the new tools, and finance draws attention to programme costs. The board has not
settled on a diagnosis or a preferred transformation approach.

Board intervention
Following a ratings-agency downgrade warning and shareholder pressure, the board removed the Chief Digital &
Technology Officer and appointed a new CEO. The CEO has 18 months to turn the business around or face a
forced sale of Auto Components. For this case, the planning period is January 2026 to June 2027, using 2025 as
the baseline.

Problem statement
The new CEO has commissioned your team to develop a digital transformation strategy that strengthens
Ironbridge’s business performance, competitiveness and long-term growth. Your recommendations should
address:
1) Identify and prioritise opportunities for digital transformation across Ironbridge’s operations and wider value
chain.
2) Recommend how digital capabilities can improve the existing business and create new avenues for growth
and differentiation.
3) Develop a phased roadmap for the CEO’s 18-month mandate, outlining the capabilities, resources and
organisational changes needed for implementation.
Assess the existing digital programme and explore additional AI and automation use cases in manufacturing and
the wider value chain. Recommendations are not limited to the three initiatives described; teams should
determine and justify their preferred scope and priorities.
