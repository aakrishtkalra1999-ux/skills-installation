## Advisor: The Contrarian

My critique went to the council as my handback. The main point: the plan doesn't pass the board's own test. Net debt rises from 755 to 816, and there's an unfunded liquidity gap of about 210 on 30 of cash. The fix I proposed is a funded liquidity bridge on slide 4.

Four other things need changing before the deck is built:
- **Benefits don't match the exhibits:** about 17 of the ~28 uplift comes from initiatives never piloted here, with no haircut. The company's own pilots delivered only 50–60% of target.
- **The cash improvement isn't digital:** about 65 of the 74 comes from working capital and cutting expansion capex.
- **SMC is contradictory:** the plan funds golden-batch in a segment facing a month-9 exit gate. Move the gate to month 6 and fund only if it passes.
- **Year one shows nothing:** 2026 is only +2 over the do-nothing case. The diagnosis should also say outright that gross-margin erosion (−43) outweighs the digital drag (−27).

## Advisor: The First Principles Thinker

I sent my First Principles Thinker view to the council.

**Main point:** The team is answering the wrong question. The deck asks "what is the best digital portfolio?" The case is really asking how Ironbridge stays solvent and keeps the Auto segment. Digital is the tool, not the goal.

- **Lead with cash, not EBITDA.** Cash is 30 against 180 of short-term debt, and interest cover is 0.6x. The deciding event is a refinancing or ratings cliff within 12 months, not the Jun-27 run-rate. By the team's own model, the plan adds only +2 EBITDA over the momentum base case in 2026. So the near-term rescue comes from the ~43 working-capital release and the capex cuts, not the digital levers.

**Four changes before the deck is built:**

1. **Put a win condition on slide 1.** Set targets for interest cover, liquidity headroom and Auto margin, taken from an external benchmark of the levels lenders refinance at for sub-investment-grade industrials. Then show the plan clears them. Right now it lands at 1.7x with no stated threshold, and admits 2.0x needs 141% benefit realisation. Judges will read that as "the plan fails."

2. **Use the EBITDA bridge to settle management's disagreement.** Of the 97 of EBITDA lost, about 27 came from digital itself: 40 of digital opex against 13 of benefits. The other ~70 came from gross-profit erosion, personnel and selling costs. That makes energy, procurement and pricing (N1, N3, N4) the core of the plan. Scaling the pilots comes second.

3. **Prioritise Auto, because Auto is the segment facing a forced sale.** The plan currently puts effort into IEM, the healthy segment: IEM-led PdM scaling and the IEM service pilot. Shift the roadmap weight to Auto's cost pass-through, energy and OEM traceability. Also move the SMC fix-or-exit decision out of "extras" and into the main plan: a segment losing 13 of EBITDA is a strategic choice, not a footnote.

4. **Name adoption as the root cause.** The pilot data shows operating-model failures, not technology failures: 60% of alerts closed on time, 35% of schedules accepted without override, and 40% of machines with central data. Eleven initiatives repeats the same mistake. Commit to five or six and stage-gate the rest.

## Advisor: The Expansionist

I've sent my Expansionist view to the council. The main point is that "Fix, Focus, Fund" is almost all cost-cutting and cash, and it barely answers part (2) of the brief (growth and differentiation). It needs a fourth pillar: "Grow". The five changes:

1. **Use the idle capacity.** Auto runs at 72% and SMC at 67%. Winning 3–5 more points of Auto utilisation through traceability and better quoting could add about +6–10 EBITDA. The team should also test making IEM's shafts and housings on idle Auto lines.
2. **Scale IEM service instead of piloting it.** Aim for 3% of IEM revenue rather than 1%. That gives about +2.4 instead of +0.8, and it is recurring revenue.
3. **Turn the forced sale of Auto into an option.** Auto EBITDA rising from 5 to about 34 creates roughly 170 of enterprise value at an assumed 6x multiple. That supports the refinancing bridge and gives the SMC fix-or-exit gate more value either way.
4. **Show what happens after Jun-27.** A 6.3% margin is still far below 2022's 10.6%. One extra line on the impact chart should show a path to about 9–10% in FY28.
5. **Present energy data as a selling point.** European OEMs increasingly ask suppliers for per-product carbon footprints, so energy management is more than a 6% saving.

The 170 figure rests on the 6x multiple, which the team would need to state as an assumption and back with a benchmark.

## Advisor: The Executor

I've sent the Executor view back to the council. In short, the reasoning holds up, but twelve initiatives are too many for the CEO to deliver in 18 months, and the plan doesn't yet say where the missing cash comes from.

- **Cut to about seven initiatives.** Drop S4, park N5 and drop N2, then merge S1 and S3 into one "make the pilots work" workstream. That gives up about 3 of the 28 uplift and makes the roadmap easier to believe.
- **Name week-1 actions and owners.** Appoint an interim digital chief or Digital Value Office head, have segment heads sign their benefit targets, switch off the planning AI layer and freeze expansion capex.
- **Start the quick levers first, in months 0–3.** Pricing, procurement and energy make up about 17 of the 28 and need no IT build; working capital adds 43 of cash. This softens the "2026 only +2 vs momentum" problem.
- **Treat the ERP move as the main schedule risk.** Roll out the three plants in waves over months 4–16 and say plainly it adds no profit inside the 18 months.
- **Fill the cash gap.** Plan FCF is −61 against 30 of cash and 180 of short-term debt. In month 1, build a 13-week cash forecast and open refinancing talks with lenders.
- **Put numbers on the gates.** For example at month 6: maintenance alerts closed on time ≥85%, vision reviews ≤25%, energy savings metered. A missed gate stops funding.

**For the deck:**
- **Slide 2:** test each manager's diagnosis against the EBITDA bridge, so one visual shows who is right about what.
- **Slide 5:** three phases (Stabilise / Prove / Scale) across the workstreams, with owners, gate KPIs and the top five risks with their triggers.

## Peer review 1 (generalist)

I've already delivered my review of the council to the caller. Here it is.

**1. Strongest: C.** It goes straight at financial feasibility, which carries 25% of the score, and every point is checked against the exhibits:
- About 17 of the 28 uplift comes from levers never piloted here (energy 5.7, procurement 5.0, pricing 6.3). The pilots only hit 50–60% of target, so C cuts those levers by the same amount.
- About 65 of the 74 FCF gain comes from working capital and capex cuts, not from digital.
- Taking inventory from 100 to 88 days needs the planning tool that just failed.
- The plan funds SMC golden-batch work before SMC's own month-9 fix-or-exit gate.

E is a close second because it recasts the problem as staying solvent and keeping Auto, not picking a digital portfolio.

**2. Biggest blind spot: B.** It adds scope: +6–10 from filling idle Auto capacity, IEM service at 3% of revenue, and selling carbon data. It never deals with the cash cliff: 30 of cash against 180 of short-term debt. It assumes Auto can win volume even though the sales head blames weak OEM demand and the pilots missed target. It also puts a 6x multiple on an unproven run-rate, the kind of unbenchmarked assumption judges mark down.

**3. What all five missed:**
- **Interest cost after refinancing.** The 1.7x cover assumes today's rate, about 5.3% (40 on 755). Refinancing at 10.7x leverage will cost more. If all 816 of debt costs 3 points more, interest rises by about 25 and cover falls to about 1.1x.
- **The baseline itself.** Nobody checked the 48 momentum base. The 23 of one-off costs said to roll off is almost as big as the plan's own +28, so it must be traced to a specific exhibit.
- **No downside case** for further loss of OEM volume.

## Peer review 4 (operations and digital practitioner)

I've sent the review back through the handback. Here's what it says:

**1. Strongest: C.** It goes after the two things that carry the most marks: financial feasibility and "consistency with exhibits." It applies the pilots' actual 50–60% realisation to about 17 of the 28 uplift that was never piloted and has no haircut. It shows that +0.5% Auto price contradicts the Auto sales head's claim of an OEM price squeeze. It notes that cutting inventory from 100 to 88 days relies on the planning tool that just failed. And it shows the cash gain comes from working capital and the capex cut, not from digital. E frames the solvency question best and A is sharpest on execution, but C's critique is closest to the judging rubric.

**2. Biggest blind spot: B.** It adds growth sized from benchmarks (3–5 points of Auto utilisation, 3% of IEM revenue from service) to a company with 30 of cash, 180 of short-term debt and 0.6x interest cover, and never mentions liquidity. More Auto volume assumes OEM demand the sales head says isn't there. Growth also ties up the working capital the plan needs to release. At 10.7x leverage, most of its "170 of value created" goes to lenders.

**3. What all five missed:**
- **Double counting.** Energy (5.7), SMC golden-batch/APC, procurement (5.0) and pricing pass-through (6.3) all come out of the same input-cost pool. Much of the energy saving likely sits in SMC, where energy is 23% of revenue. Exiting SMC at month 9 would wipe that part out.
- **Plant footprint.** The three segments run at 67–81% utilisation, with Auto at 72% and SMC at 67%. Closing or merging one Auto or SMC plant could likely beat any single digital lever. The machine-efficiency (OEE) data the digital programme collects is what should pick the plant.
- **Contract exit costs.** Most of the 48 of digital opex is probably on multi-year vendor contracts. Nobody costed the termination penalties behind switching off the LLM planning layer (+2.9).
