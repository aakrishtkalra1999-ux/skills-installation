Our answer: fix digital so it pays for itself, and Ironbridge passes all four turnaround tests by June 2027

The problem

  • EBITDA fell from 122 to 25 between 2022 and 2025. Ironbridge now earns less than its interest bill (cover of 0.6x), owes 30 times its EBITDA, and has 30 in cash against 180 of debt due this year.
  • About 70 of the 97 lost came from the core business: car makers pushing prices down, higher input and energy costs, and rising overheads. About 27 was self-inflicted: the digital programme costs 48 a year and saves only 13.
  • The tools are not the problem. People are not using them: only 60% of maintenance alerts are acted on in time, planners accept just 35% of the AI's schedules, and 3 plants are not on the shared ERP.

What we recommend: Fix, Focus, Fund

  • Fix: switch off the AI agent in the planning tool, clean up master data, and move every plant onto one ERP. A small Digital Value Office under the CEO releases money only when checkpoints are met.
  • Focus: make the two working pilots pay before growing them, and aim digital at the biggest costs: energy, purchasing, passing cost increases on to customers, and back-office work.
  • Fund: hold back expansion spending (no plant runs above 81% of capacity), free up 38 of cash tied up in stock and unpaid invoices, and cover the gap with asset sales, a sale-and-leaseback and receivables finance.
By June 2027, annual EBITDA reaches 69, against 48 if nothing changes (+21 from our plan). The 18-month cash burn is cut in half (−74 against −142), and Auto's margin is back to 5.0% from 0.9%.

Roadmap: three phases over 18 months with two checkpoints. Business heads own the results, and money stops wherever a checkpoint is missed

18-month plan, January 2026 to June 2027

Checkpoint 1 (month 6): at least 85% of maintenance alerts closed on time, no more than 25% of vision rejects re-checked by hand, energy savings measured, and SMC prices on track for +3.6% (if not, start the exit). Checkpoint 2 (month 9): a named car maker needs traceability, the second batch of machines is released, and interest cover over the last 12 months is heading to 1.0x.

Who runs it

  • A Chief Digital Officer and a small Digital Value Office (about 5 people) report to the CEO. They track savings and release money only at checkpoints.
  • Each business head signs up to their savings target. Every workstream is led by someone from the business, not from IT.
  • The central digital team of 24 moves into the workstreams. Each plant gets a digital champion, and we add technicians to act on maintenance alerts.
  • Week 1: hire the CDO, freeze expansion spending, start weekly cash control, and give notice on the AI agent.
  • 18-month budget: 10 of set-up costs and 29 of digital capex (30 was requested), released in stages.

How we stay consistent with the case

  • The 23 of one-off costs that stop is Exhibit 6's set-up spend (7 + 8 + 8). Only 6 of remaining work carries into 2026, and it is in both cases.
  • The 13 of 2025 savings is already in cost of sales, so we never count it again (Exhibit 5).
  • The 38 of cash from stock and invoices is one-off, and kept apart from yearly EBITDA (Exhibit 7).
  • The vision pilot's 7 of savings from a 1.2-point scrap cut would mean 583 of production on 8 lines, 63% of the Group's cost of sales. That cannot be right, so we do not scale it up that way.
  • Maintenance capex and the 12 of central digital cost stay flat, because the case asks for a reason to cut them.
