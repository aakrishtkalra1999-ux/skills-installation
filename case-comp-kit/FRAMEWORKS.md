# Frameworks guide

A working summary of the structures taught in the four casebooks (IIMA, IIM Calcutta, FMS, ISB), written in our own words, plus how to use each one in a **written case competition**, where you have days instead of minutes. Page pointers go to `reference/CASEBOOKS.md`.

## The ground rules every book agrees on
- **Clarify before you structure.** Restate the problem, ask what success looks like, what time frame, what constraints. In a competition, read the brief's evaluation criteria and slide limits as part of this.
- **Structure to fit the problem, don't force a template.** Frameworks are starting checklists. Judges and interviewers reward a clean issue tree built for this case.
- **MECE.** Every split should have no overlaps and no gaps (by formula, by process step, by segment, by internal vs external).
- **Hypothesis first.** Say what you think is going on, then use data to confirm or kill it.
- **Two comparisons for every number:** versus its own past, and versus competitors.
- **Sanity check** any estimate with a second method (top-down vs bottom-up).
- **End with a clear recommendation, a plan and risks,** not a list of considerations.

## Scoping a messy problem (ISB p33, p49)
Before any framework, pin down: **objective** (what metric, by how much, by when), **context** (company, market, recent events), **constraints** (money, time, regulation), **stakeholders** (who decides, who is affected) and **scope** (what is in and out). For abstract or public-sector problems, ask the four Ws: who is affected, what exactly is the problem, where, and when or since when, then how to fix it.

## Profitability (IIMA p14, IIM C p42 to 47, FMS p53, ISB p34 to 36)
Profit = revenue − cost.
- **Revenue:** price × volume, split by product, customer segment, geography and channel. Volume = market size × share, or customers × frequency.
- **Cost:** fixed vs variable, then by value-chain step (raw material, production, distribution, selling, overheads).
- **Customer-journey lens** for revenue drops (IIM C): awareness, consideration, purchase, repeat. Find where people fall away.
- **Common traps:** a mix shift that looks like a price problem; one segment hiding inside a group average; cost per unit rising because volume fell (fixed-cost absorption).
- **In a competition:** build the bridge of the headline metric from the first year to the last (Armageddon's EBITDA bridge). Rule on each stakeholder's view with numbers. Separate one-offs from structural problems.

## Market entry and go-to-market (IIMA p14, IIM C p156, FMS p53 to 57, ISB p39 to 42)
Four questions in order:
1. **Why enter?** Growth, diversification, following customers, using spare capacity.
2. **Is the market attractive?** Size, growth, margins, competition (Porter), regulation and risks (PESTEL).
3. **Can the client win?** Capabilities, cost position, brand, right to play; customer needs vs the offer.
4. **How to enter?** Build, buy, partner or license, then the economics (investment, break-even, payback), then go or no-go.
- GTM adds: target segment, value proposition, pricing, channels, launch sequence, and localisation (culture, regulation).
- **In a competition:** size the market bottom-up and top-down, show break-even and payback, give a phased entry with checkpoints.

## Pricing (IIMA p14, IIM C p231, FMS p56, ISB p37 to 38)
Three anchors, then choose:
- **Cost-based:** the floor (unit cost plus target margin).
- **Competitor-based:** where substitutes sit.
- **Value-based:** the ceiling (what the customer gains or saves, and willingness to pay).
- New products: start from unit economics and value. Existing products: price elasticity, how competitors respond, cannibalisation.
- Models: one-off vs subscription, tiers, bundles, dynamic pricing, freemium, auctions and bids (IPL bidding, sports rights).
- **In a competition:** show the price ladder (cost floor, competitor band, value ceiling) on one chart and test volume sensitivity.

## Growth strategy (IIMA p14, IIM C p263 to 264, FMS p55, ISB p43 to 45)
- **Ansoff matrix:** existing or new products × existing or new markets (penetration, product development, market development, diversification).
- **Organic vs inorganic:** build, partner, acquire.
- **Three steps:** list the options, size each one, prioritise by value, feasibility, time and risk.
- International expansion adds country screening: size, ease of doing business, distance (cultural, administrative, geographic, economic).
- **In a competition:** a value-vs-feasibility (or value-vs-speed) matrix with sized bubbles, then a sequenced roadmap.

## M&A, PE/VC and due diligence (IIMA p14 to 31, IIM C p336, FMS p58 to 59, ISB p46 to 48)
- **Rationale:** why buy, and why this target (growth, capability, synergy, defence)?
- **Target's market:** size, growth, profitability, competition.
- **Target itself:** financials, operations, team, customers, moat.
- **Fit and synergies:** revenue synergies (cross-selling, new markets) and cost synergies (scale, overlap), cultural and strategic fit.
- **Valuation and deal:** DCF, multiples, comparable deals; price vs value plus synergies; financing; exit options for PE.
- **Risks and integration:** regulation (PESTEL), integration plan, first-100-days.
- **Commercial due diligence** (PE asks consultants): market, competition, business, customers.
- **In a competition:** show a sources-and-uses and a value bridge (standalone value + synergies − integration cost − premium paid).

## Operations, digital and unconventional cases (IIMA p215, IIM C p368, ISB p49 to 52)
- **Operations:** map the process step by step, find the bottleneck (capacity, utilisation, downtime, quality), fix the constraint first.
- **Digital transformation:** split front-end (customer, sales, pricing) and back-end (operations, supply chain, finance). Ask what problem each tool solves and whether people will use it. Adoption and data quality usually decide success (this was Armageddon's root cause).
- **Public policy and social:** stakeholder map, root-cause tree, interventions sorted by impact and ease, governance and monitoring. ISB's governance trees (p51 to 52) show how to break a public-service problem down.
- **Sales effectiveness:** number of people × productivity (skill, will, time spent selling, tools).
- **Abstract cases** (dog purchase, red pandas, church footfall): drop the business template and build the tree from first principles with the four Ws.

## Guesstimates (IIMA p297, IIM C p433, FMS p38 to 52, ISB p141 to 158)
1. Clarify the definition (units, geography, time period).
2. Choose top-down (population, then filters) or bottom-up (one store or one household, then scale up).
3. Split on the drivers that matter (urban vs rural, income, age, usage frequency).
4. State every assumption, calculate, then sanity-check with the other method.
- Cheat sheets with India population splits: ISB p144, FMS p47, IIMA datasheets p386, IIM C p451. Verify before quoting in a deliverable.

## Strategy tools worth knowing
- **Porter's five forces:** how attractive an industry is (IIMA p14 to 31, IIM C p36).
- **PESTEL:** external risks and drivers.
- **SWOT:** a summary, rarely the analysis itself.
- **BCG matrix:** portfolio by growth and share (FMS p18).
- **Ansoff:** growth paths (FMS p22).
- **McKinsey 7S:** organisational alignment for change and integration (FMS p24).
- **Value chain:** where cost and value sit (FMS p21).
- **VRIO:** whether an advantage lasts (FMS p63, ISB p53).
- **ROIC tree:** links operations to returns (FMS p34).
- **Valuation basics:** FMS p35 to 37.

## From interview framework to winning written answer
Interview frameworks get you a structure. Competitions also reward:
1. **A numbers backbone:** a reconciled model; every claim traced to an exhibit or a stated assumption.
2. **Cash and risk, not just profit:** funding, phasing, downside cases.
3. **A clear answer on slide 1,** with tests it passes.
4. **Honest phasing:** set-up costs before savings, checkpoints that stop the money if targets are missed.
5. **Plain writing:** one message per slide, headline as a full sentence.
