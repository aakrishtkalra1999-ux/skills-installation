INDUSTRY OVERVIEW
India’s small and marginal farmers often sell individually,
with limited access to buyers, finance and reliable
agricultural services. Small production volumes and
fragmented market access weaken their bargaining
power and make it difficult to secure favourable prices.

Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs) bring farmers
together to aggregate produce, purchase inputs
collectively and access larger markets. However, many
struggle to generate enough business to cover operating
costs and deliver consistent value to their members.
Establishing an FPO is therefore only the first step towards
building a commercially viable organisation.

Cluster-Based Business Organisations (CBBOs) support
FPOs through business development, market linkages,
professional management and institutional strengthening.
Their role is to help FPOs develop both the business
volumes and internal capabilities needed to operate
independently once the initial period of external support
ends.
CASE BACKGROUND
A philanthropic Foundation has committed US$1 million
annually for three to five years to strengthen FPOs in Bihar
through Kosi Agri Partners (KAP). KAP is a CBBO supporting
12 FPOs across flood-prone districts of north and north-
east Bihar, of which seven focus on maize and five on
vegetables.

A typical FPO was established in 2022–23 and has
approximately 500 members, around 20% of whom are
women. It generates annual turnover of ₹20–35 lakh, with
each member having contributed ₹2,000 in share capital.
Its nine-member board typically includes one woman
director.

Despite this foundation, the FPOs continue to face
constraints across market access, working capital,
infrastructure and management. With KAP’s handholding
expected to conclude around 2027–28, the Foundation
must determine how its funding can help these
organisations build viable businesses and reduce their
dependence on external support.
     KEY CHALLENGES

Market Access and Business Development: The FPOs
rely largely on local traders and a small number of buyers,
with no formal supply contracts. Low produce
aggregation volumes limit their ability to serve larger
buyers and negotiate favourable terms. The challenge is
to build reliable buyer relationships and increase the
volume of business conducted through the FPOs.



Financial Access: A typical FPO has approximately ₹10
lakh in working capital against peak-season requirements
of ₹25 lakh. Limited access to credit, combined with
buyer payment delays of 30–60 days, restricts
procurement and makes timely farmer payments difficult.
The FPOs need financing arrangements that support
seasonal business requirements without creating an
unsustainable repayment burden.
     KEY CHALLENGES
Operational Infrastructure and Supply Chains:
Limited access to storage, grading, quality-control
facilities and reliable logistics affects the FPOs’ ability to
handle produce efficiently and meet buyer requirements.
Flood and drought risks create further uncertainty
around procurement and delivery. KAP must determine
which operational improvements are commercially
justified, accounting for the different handling
requirements of maize and vegetables.

Organisational Capacity and Farmer Participation:
Gaps in professional management, accounting,
governance and compliance limit the FPOs’ ability to
operate independently. Weak member engagement can
also restrict participation in procurement and sales
activities. Women account for around 20% of
membership, while a typical nine-member board includes
only one woman director. Strengthening internal
capabilities and farmer participation will be essential to
sustaining operations after KAP’s support ends.
         DELIVERABLES
Strategic Priorities: Identify two or three challenges that
KAP should prioritise to improve FPO sustainability. Explain
why addressing these constraints would have the greatest
impact on business performance and independent
operations. Distinguish between priorities common to all 12
FPOs and those requiring different approaches for maize-
and vegetable-focused FPOs. A detailed assessment of
individual FPOs is not required.

Priority Interventions and Expected Outcomes:
Recommend up to three practical interventions to address
the priorities identified above. Explain how KAP should
implement each intervention and where the approach
should differ between maize- and vegetable-focused
FPOs. Define the expected outcomes by the end of the
support period using a few measurable targets, such as
farmer participation, business volumes or the ability to
cover operating costs. Clearly state the assumptions
supporting these targets.
